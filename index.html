K-Pickle

Cucumbers, salt, and time. Nothing in this jar that K's grandmother wouldn't recognise.

Follow the brine

One — The Garden

It starts in the dirt.

Four acres of river-bottom loam on the slow side of Hollis Bend. We cut Kirbys at first light, while the skins still squeak and the vines are cold to the hand.

Nothing we grow rides in a truck for more than an hour. A cucumber picked yesterday is already a different vegetable, and it will taste like one.

  • Picked at dawn
  • In brine by noon
  • Four acres, no more

Two — The Brine

Nothing in it you can't name.

White vinegar and well water, cut bright with fresh lime juice. Garlic crushed under the flat of a knife, whole black peppercorns, a fistful of dill, and kosher salt — the only cure this brine needs.

K's grandmother wrote the ratio on the back of a seed catalogue in 1962. We have never found a reason to improve it.

  • White vinegar & lime juice
  • Certified kosher
  • Dill & black pepper

Three — The Crock

Then we leave it alone.

Packed into stoneware crocks, cucumbers going in whole, the brine poured over cold and the lid set on top.

Five days is all it takes for the vinegar and lime to work all the way through — long enough for the garlic and dill to settle in, not so long the snap goes soft.

01 days in the brine

Four — The Jar

Packed by hand, one jar at a time.

Cold and upright, whole cucumbers set against the glass so the brine can still move between them. Never pasteurised — heat would buy us a longer shelf life and cost us the snap.

Every lid goes on by hand. Every label is dated by the person who filled it. If a jar isn't right, it doesn't leave the room.

  • Hand-packed
  • Dated & signed
  • Kept cold, always

Five — The First Bite

Open it cold.

The snap comes first, then the bright hit of lime, then the slow heat of the garlic somewhere behind your teeth. Eat them standing at the sink, the way K does.

And keep the brine. It is the best thing in the jar and everybody pours it down the drain.
